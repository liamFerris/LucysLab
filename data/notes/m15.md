# Module 15: Other intra-oral radiographic techniques

*Practical tips to overcome positioning difficulties*

## What you should be able to do

- Describe the intra-oral radiographic techniques for imaging lower third molars.
- Describe the methods of X-raying patients with a strong gag reflex.
- Describe the radiographic techniques for edentulous and partially edentulous patients.
- Explain how radiographs are obtained during endodontics.
- Explain how to modify intra-oral techniques when X-raying small children.

## Lower third molars (wisdom teeth)

**Problem:** <mark>positioning the film packet or digital sensor in a paralleling technique holder far enough back in the mouth</mark>.

**Solution:** <mark>use specially designed or adapted holders</mark> to position the sensor/film at the back of the mouth.

**Problem:** being able to record <mark>the entire tooth and the surrounding tissues, including the inferior (dental) canal</mark>, on the same image.

**Solution:** <mark>take two periapical views</mark> with the image receptor positioned as far posteriorly as possible, but <mark>using two different X-ray tubehead positions</mark>.

## Presence of a strong gag reflex

**Problem:** placement of the image receptor in the desired position, <mark>especially in the upper and lower posterior molar regions</mark>.

**Solution 1:** ask the patient to <mark>suck a local anaesthetic lozenge</mark> before attempting to position the image receptor.

**Solution 2:** ask the patient to <mark>concentrate on breathing deeply</mark> while the image receptor is in the mouth.

**Solution 3:** <mark>place the image receptor flat in the mouth (in the occlusal plane)</mark> so that it does not touch the palate.

## Edentulous or partially edentulous patients

**Problem:** placing the image receptor in the ideal position because of:

- lack of height in the palate;
- loss of lingual sulcus depth;
- rotation or displacement of the image receptor holder.

**Solution 1:** take intra-oral periapicals using a <mark>modified bisected angle technique</mark>.

**Solution 2:** in partially dentate patients, <mark>build up the deficiency using cotton wool rolls</mark>.

## Radiography during endodontics

**Problem:** inability to place image receptors and to stabilise them when <mark>endodontic instruments, rubber dam and rubber dam clamps</mark> are in position.

**Solution 1:** use a <mark>simple phosphor plate/film packet holder</mark>, positioned in the mouth parallel to the tooth and held in place by the patient.

**Solution 2:** use one of the <mark>special endodontic film or sensor holders</mark>.

**Problem:** <mark>identification and separation of root canals in multi-rooted teeth</mark>.

**Solution:** take <mark>at least two or even three radiographs</mark> of the multi-rooted tooth <mark>using different horizontal X-ray tubehead positions</mark>.

## Small children

**Problem:** placing image receptors in the ideal position because of:

- size of mouth;
- co-operation.

**Solution 1:** <mark>place the image receptor flat in the mouth (in the occlusal plane)</mark> and use a <mark>modified bisected angle technique</mark>.

**Solution 2:** use <mark>alternative extra-oral radiographs (oblique laterals)</mark>.
