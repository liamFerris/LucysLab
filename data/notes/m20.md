# Module 20: How to take cephalometric lateral skull radiographs for orthodontics

*Practical techniques*

## What you should be able to do

- Explain the terms "true cephalometric lateral skull" and "cephalometry".
- Describe the clinical indications.
- Describe the geometrical requirements.
- Describe the equipment required and the practical technique for taking a cephalometric lateral skull.
- Describe the ideal image quality criteria.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Cephalo* | <mark>In or of the head.</mark> |
| *Metric* | <mark>Measurement.</mark> |
| Cephalometry | <mark>The measurement and comparison of specific points, distances and lines within the facial skeleton.</mark> |

<mark>True cephalometric lateral skull radiographs are used to assess the relationships of the teeth to the jaws, and the jaws to the rest of the facial skeleton.</mark> They are used mainly in orthodontics.

> **Lucy's note:** Also called a "lateral ceph" or "lateral skull".

## Clinical indications for a true cephalometric lateral skull

- Initial orthodontic diagnosis.

> **Lucy's note:** Confirmation of underlying dental, skeletal and/or soft tissue abnormalities.

- Treatment planning.
- Monitoring treatment progress.
- To assist in the localisation and assessment of unerupted, malformed or misplaced teeth.
- At the end of active treatment, to check that treatment targets have been achieved.
- At the end of retention, if:
  - stability is uncertain;
  - unfavourable growth is indicated;
  - there are clinically observed changes, in order to provide a baseline record.

## Geometrical requirements

- The equipment needs to be able to ensure the precise alignment of the X-ray beam, the image receptor and the patient.
- To minimise magnification effects, the focus-to-image receptor distance should ideally be between 1.5 and 1.8 metres.

## Equipment required

- X-ray machine: a separate skull unit, or a panoramic unit.
- Image receptor: film/cassette, CCD or phosphor plate.
- Cephalostat: a head positioning and stabilising device (a separate unit, or an attachment to a panoramic unit).

## Technique and positioning

The technique is the same for both types of combined panoramic/ceph unit. The only difference is that with a "one shot" unit you insert a cassette.

| Combined "one shot" panoramic/ceph unit | Combined "scanning" panoramic/ceph unit |
|---|---|
| Image receptor: phosphor plate or film | Image receptor: solid state |
| Cling film the ear rods and insert the cassette into the cephalostat. | Cling film the ear rods. |

Then, for either unit:

1. Fit the thyroid collar.
2. Position the patient carefully in the cephalostat, with the sagittal plane vertical and parallel to the image receptor, and with the Frankfort plane horizontal.
3. Immobilise the head with the plastic ear rods, inserted gradually and **very carefully** into the external auditory meati, and position the nasion marker on the bridge of the nose.
4. Ask the patient to bite together with their teeth in maximum intercuspation.
5. The X-ray tubehead should be in its fixed position relative to the cephalostat (1.5–1.8 m).
6. Ensure correct collimation is in place.
7. Make the exposure.

## Ideal image quality criteria

- Facial skeleton and cranial base included on the image and clearly defined.
- Teeth together and in maximum intercuspation.
- Right and left ear rod shadows superimposed exactly.
- Soft tissue profile identifiable (digital enhancement).
- Frankfort plane horizontal.
- Minimal geometrical distortion.
- No unwanted superimposition of adjacent structures.
- Contrast and density such that hard and soft tissues can be differentiated and all orthodontic points are identifiable.
