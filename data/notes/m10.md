# Module 10: Patient care and infection control

*How should you look after patients during dental radiography and what control of infection measures should you use?*

## What you should be able to do

- Describe the general guidelines for caring for patients, both adults and children, undergoing dental radiography.
- Explain why control of infection precautions are needed during dental radiography.
- Describe the practical control of infection procedures needed during both intra-oral and panoramic radiography.

## Patient care

### Patient preparation checklist

- Confirm the patient's identity.
- Check their current medical history.
- Confirm the radiographic request.
- Explain the procedure.
- Reassure the patient.
- Remove spectacles, dentures, orthodontic appliances and relevant jewellery.

### General considerations

- Position the patient comfortably with their head supported.
- If the patient is sitting upright, make sure the occlusal plane is horizontal and parallel to the floor.
- If the patient is lying down, modify the techniques to suit.
- Use a thyroid shield as appropriate.
- Select the exposure before positioning the film packet and tubehead.
- Position intra-oral film packets carefully to avoid soft tissue trauma.
- Carry out the procedure quickly and accurately.
- Always watch the patient throughout the exposure.

### Children

- Reassure the child and explain the procedure.
- Use appropriate techniques and film sizes.
- Use thyroid protection.
- If necessary, use an accompanying carer or comforter to help. Provide the carer with a lead apron.
- Use oblique laterals for extra-oral views.

## Intra-oral radiography: infection control checklists

### Pre-operative

- Check the current medical history.
- Wear latex or vinyl gloves and eye protection.
- Use barrier-wrapped film packets, or digital phosphor plates in their own barrier envelopes.
- Cover CCD digital sensors with suitable plastic barrier coverings.
- Consider using disposable polystyrene bite blocks.
- Place all required film packets (or sensors) and all required holders on a disposable tray or paper towel.
- Consider covering the tubehead and control panel in cling film to act as a physical barrier.

### Post-operative

1. Return film packets and holders to the tray or towel.
2. Open the barrier envelope without touching the film and drop the film onto the work surface.
3. Dispose of the barrier envelope and tray as clinical waste.
4. Rinse saliva off the film holders.
5. Wash hands.
6. Scrub the film holders in soapy water and rinse.
7. Disinfect the beam aiming ring.
8. Autoclave the film holders.
9. Then deal with the tubehead and controls in one of two ways:
   - **If cling film was used:** wash gloved hands, remove the contaminated cling film, and discard the cling film and gloves as clinical waste.
   - **Or, if no cling film was used:** wipe down the X-ray tubehead, control panel and timer button with surface disinfectant, then discard the gloves as clinical waste.

### Daylight loading automatic processors

- Remove gloves first.
- Do not place saliva-contaminated film packets or gloved hands into these units.

## Panoramic radiography: infection control checklists

### Pre-operative

- Check the current medical history.
- Put on latex or vinyl gloves.
- Insert a sterile bite peg into the holder.
- Cover the control panel and exposure button (high-risk zones) in cling film to act as a physical barrier.
- If using a cassette, cover it in cling film.

### Post-operative

- Rinse and wash the bite peg, then autoclave it.
- Remove the cling film from the control panel.
- Wash gloved hands.
- Wipe down the control panel, handles, chin support and head supports with surface disinfectant.
- Remove the cling film from the cassette (if appropriate).
- Discard gloves as clinical waste.

## Lateral skull radiography: recommendations

Lateral skull radiography is covered in Module 20.

### Pre-operative

- Check the current medical history.
- Wear appropriate PPE.
- If using a cassette, cover it in cling film and position it in the unit.
- Cover the ear rods in cling film.
- Cover the control panel and exposure button (high-risk zones) in cling film to act as a physical barrier.

### Post-operative

- Remove the cassette from the unit.
- Remove the cling film from the ear rods.
- Remove the cling film from the control panel.
- Wipe down the ear rods and nasion marker.
- Wipe down the control panels.
