# Module 2: What is an X-ray?

*The nature and dangers of X-rays*

## What you should be able to do

- Explain the electromagnetic spectrum.
- Describe the difference between X-rays and gamma rays.
- Classify and understand the dangers associated with ionising radiation.

## Electromagnetic radiation

<mark>Photons are a type of electromagnetic radiation. They come in a wide range of energies, which determine their properties. Very low energy photons make up radio waves, whilst photons with medium energy are visible to us as light.</mark>

Gamma ray photons and X-ray photons have <mark>high energy</mark> compared with other types of electromagnetic radiation. <mark>They can also be classified as ionising radiation.</mark>

### X-rays and gamma rays

<mark>Gamma rays and X-rays are the same thing.</mark> <mark>The only difference is that gamma rays are naturally occurring, whilst X-rays are man-made.</mark>

## How X-ray photons interact with matter

X-ray photons can interact with matter in four main ways:

| Interaction | What happens |
|---|---|
| <mark>Transmission</mark> | The photon passes straight through the matter without touching anything. |
| <mark>Absorption</mark> | The photon hits the matter and all its energy is absorbed. |
| <mark>Absorption and scatter</mark> | The photon hits the matter and is deflected in another direction, with a loss of energy. |
| <mark>Pure scatter</mark> | The photon is deflected in another direction with no loss of energy. |

## Main properties of X-rays

- <mark>An X-ray is a packet of energy called a photon.</mark>
- <mark>An X-ray beam will contain millions of photons.</mark>
- <mark>They travel in straight lines.</mark>
- <mark>They can travel in a vacuum.</mark>
- <mark>X-rays have a short wavelength and high energy.</mark>
- <mark>They originate at the atomic level.</mark>
- <mark>They interact with matter at the atomic level.</mark>
- <mark>They can blacken film emulsion to create an image.</mark>
- <mark>They cannot be detected by any human sense.</mark>
- <mark>They can damage human tissue.</mark>

## Classification of the dangers of ionising radiation

### Tissue reaction effects (somatic, deterministic or certainty effects)

These are certainty effects which result from high doses of radiation. <mark>Normal body (somatic) cells are affected and the severity of the damage is proportional to the radiation dose.</mark> <mark>Dental radiography never uses radiation doses high enough to cause these effects.</mark>

### Stochastic effects

Stochastic effects are chance effects. There are two kinds.

**<mark>Somatic</mark> stochastic effects:** chance effects which might result from any dose of radiation. Normal body (somatic) cells are affected, but the severity of the damage bears no relation to the radiation dose. Although very unlikely, a single X-ray photon has the potential to cause a fatal cancer. Increasing the radiation dose increases the chance of damage occurring.

**<mark>Genetic</mark> stochastic effects:** these chance effects happen to the reproductive cells (egg and sperm). If damage to these cells occurs, it may show up in future offspring. Again, these effects can occur after any exposure to radiation. They are very unlikely to result from dental radiography, as the X-ray beam is never aimed at the reproductive organs.

<mark>There is no such thing as a safe dose of radiation.</mark>

## The aims of radiation protection

<mark>The main aims of radiation protection are to completely prevent the deterministic effects and to limit the chances of stochastic effects happening to an acceptable level.</mark>
