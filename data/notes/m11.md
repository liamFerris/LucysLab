# Module 11: How to take paralleling technique periapical radiographs

*Practical techniques*

## What you should be able to do

- Define the terms periapical and paralleling.
- Describe the clinical indications for periapical radiography.
- Describe the geometrical requirements of the paralleling technique.
- Describe the practical techniques.
- Explain the technique's advantages and disadvantages.
- Describe the ideal periapical image quality criteria.

## What is periapical paralleling radiography?

<mark>Periapical radiography is designed to show individual teeth and the tissues around the apices.</mark> <mark>With the paralleling technique, the image receptor is positioned parallel to the long axis of the tooth.</mark>

## Geometrical requirements

- The image receptor is parallel to the long axis of the tooth, and the X-ray beam hits the receptor at 90°.
- The image receptor position is ideally achieved using sensor/film holders.
- A long focus-to-skin distance (fsd) of 200 mm keeps divergence of the X-ray beam (and therefore magnification of the image) to a minimum.
- The X-ray beam position is achieved by the beam aiming device.

## Clinical indications for taking a periapical radiograph

- Detection of apical infection/inflammation and other diseases
- Assessment of periodontal status
- After trauma to the teeth and associated alveolar bone
- Assessment of root shape and number before extractions
- Before, during and after endodontics
- Before and after apical surgery
- Post-operative evaluation of implants

## Setting up image receptor holders and beam aiming devices

| | Anterior teeth | Posterior teeth |
|---|---|---|
| Biteblock | Small (blue) | Large (yellow) |
| Metal arm | Anterior | Posterior |
| Aiming ring | Blue | Aiming ring (posterior set-up) |
| Image receptor | Small intra-oral phosphor plate or film packet | Large intra-oral phosphor plate or film packet |
| Receptor orientation | Long axis vertical | Long axis horizontal |

### Anterior teeth

- Select the small (blue) biteblock, anterior metal arm and (blue) aiming ring, and connect them together.
- Insert a small intra-oral phosphor plate or intra-oral film packet with its long axis vertical.

### Posterior teeth

- Select the large (yellow) biteblock, posterior metal arm and aiming ring.
- Connect them together, set up for right or left side and for mandible or maxilla.
- Insert a large intra-oral phosphor plate or film packet with its long axis horizontal.
- Check the set-up by looking through the aiming ring.

When using solid-state digital sensors, modify the technique to accommodate the sensor cable, and make sure the sensor has a barrier sheath fitted.

## Advantages of the paralleling technique

- Geometrically accurate images with minimal magnification.
- Minimal elongation or foreshortening.
- Crowns, roots and periodontal bone levels are well represented.
- The position of the X-ray tubehead is determined by the aiming device.
- The X-ray beam is aimed at the centre of the image receptor, so there is no coning off (cone cutting).
- Reproducible images are possible at different visits and by different operators.

## Disadvantages of the paralleling technique

- Positioning the image receptor can be uncomfortable, sometimes causing gagging.
- Positioning the holders can be difficult for inexperienced operators.
- The anatomy of the mouth (e.g. a flat, shallow palate) can make the technique impossible.
- Positioning the holders in the lower third molar region can be difficult.
- The technique is impossible in very small children.
- The technique needs to be modified during endodontics (see Module 15).

## Periapical ideal quality criteria

- The crown and entire roots of the tooth/teeth under investigation are included on the image.
- The apical tissues are clearly defined, including the full extent of any disease present.
- Minimal vertical geometrical distortion in tooth length.
- No overlapping.
- No unwanted superimposition of adjacent structures.
- Contrast and density are such that enamel, dentine and pulp can be differentiated.
