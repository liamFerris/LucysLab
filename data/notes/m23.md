# Module 23: The law

*Legal requirements and recommended good practice*

## What you should be able to do

- Describe the essential requirements of the legislation governing the use of ionising radiation.
- Explain the role and responsibilities of a dental care professional acting as the operator.
- Explain the importance of consent in relation to radiography.

## The two sets of legislation

<mark>There are two sets of legislation governing the use of radiation in dental practice:</mark>

| Legislation | Concerned with |
|---|---|
| <mark>Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 (IRR17)</mark> | The safety of **workers** and the **general public** |
| <mark>Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017 (IRMER)</mark> | The **safety of patients**, and ensuring that they benefit from X-ray investigations |

## Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 (IRR)

### Essential legal requirements

<mark>The employer needs to:</mark>

- apply to the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) to register to use ionising radiation;
- carry out a prior radiation risk assessment;
- restrict exposure (limitation and ALARP);
- obtain the "critical examination" and "acceptance test" reports on installation of new equipment;
- maintain equipment;
- have contingency plans in the event of equipment malfunction and significant or unintended exposures;
- consult with a Radiation Protection Adviser (RPA);
- ensure all staff are adequately informed, instructed and trained;
- designate the "controlled area" or areas;
- appoint a Radiation Protection Supervisor (RPS);
- produce and update "Local Rules";
- instigate a Quality Assurance programme;
- ensure employees are aware of their legal duties.

### Duties of employees

- Employees should not knowingly expose themselves or any other person to X-rays to an extent greater than necessary for the purposes of their work.
- Employees must exercise reasonable care when working with any aspect of dental radiology.
- Employees must immediately report to their employer whenever they have reasonable cause to believe that an incident or accident has occurred leading to an overexposure.

## Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017 (IRMER)

### Essential legal requirements

- Providing the "written procedures" for all medical exposures and ensuring compliance.
- Ensuring all Practitioners and Operators are "adequately trained", undertake continuing education and training after qualification, and maintain appropriate records.
- Ensuring all medical exposures are justified.
- Ensuring all doses are kept as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP).
- Appointing or consulting with a Medical Physics Expert (MPE) to advise on dosimetry, equipment QA, dose, optimisation and radiation protection.
- Providing a process for dealing with accidental and unintended exposures.
- Providing a Quality Assurance programme, including for equipment.
- Ensuring clinical audit is undertaken.

### The IRMER Operator

- An Operator is a person conducting any practical aspect of a medical exposure, including exposing the radiograph and processing the film.
- To ensure that patients benefit from being X-rayed, Operators must be adequately trained for the role they play during the exposure.
- Dental nurses acting as Operators involved in X-raying patients should possess a Certificate in Dental Radiography. This course is designed to prepare you for the BDA Certificate in Dental Radiography.
- Operators involved in processing or QA should have a Certificate in Dental Radiography, or have received adequate and documented training specific to the tasks that they undertake.
- Continuing Professional Development (CPD) is mandatory for all Practitioners and Operators. Operators should attend a continuing education course every five years.

## Consent

You, as the Operator, have to verify that the patient has given verbal or written consent to being X-rayed. Consent must be **voluntary** and **informed**, and the patient must have the **capacity** to understand the information provided. If you were present in the surgery when the patient gave consent, you can proceed.

<mark>If you were not present, but have been asked by your dentist to X-ray a patient, then it would be good practice to:</mark>

- <mark>confirm the patient's identity;</mark>
- <mark>confirm with the patient which radiographs you will be taking;</mark>
- <mark>confirm with the patient that they are happy to proceed and that they have read the patient information leaflet or poster;</mark>
- <mark>confirm that you have parental consent before X-raying children.</mark>
