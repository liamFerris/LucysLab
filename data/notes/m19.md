# Module 19: How do you take oblique lateral radiographs?

*Practical techniques*

## What you should be able to do

- Define the term oblique lateral radiography and name the different lateral projections.
- Describe the clinical indications for oblique lateral radiographs.
- Describe the geometrical requirements and practical techniques for the various oblique lateral projections taken of adults and children.

## Types of lateral radiograph

Lateral radiographs are side views of the head and jaws.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| True laterals | <mark>The image receptor is parallel to the sagittal plane, i.e. front-to-back.</mark> |
| Oblique laterals | <mark>The image receptor is not parallel to the sagittal plane, i.e. it is at an oblique angle.</mark> |
| Bimolars | <mark>Two oblique laterals on one film.</mark> |

## Clinical indications for an oblique lateral radiograph

- Assessment of the presence and/or position of unerupted teeth, particularly wisdom teeth.
- Detection of fractures of the mandible.
- As an alternative to intra-oral views when these are unobtainable, e.g. in children.
- Evaluation of lesions or conditions affecting the jaws, such as cysts or tumours.
- As a specific view of the mandibular condyle.

## Geometrical requirements and practical techniques

The view shows the upper and lower teeth of one side.

- The patient sits upright and holds a cassette against the side of the face, touching the cheek; the chin is raised and the head rotated.
- The position of the cassette is determined by the area of interest.
- The X-ray beam is aimed at the area of interest:
  - **To image the right molars**, the X-ray tubehead is positioned along the line of the occlusal plane, just below the ear and behind the ascending ramus, aiming through the radiographic keyhole and the molar teeth.
  - **To image the right canines/premolars**, the X-ray tubehead is positioned along the line of the occlusal plane, just below the ear and behind the ascending ramus, aiming through the radiographic keyhole at the canine and premolar teeth.

### Alternative technique

- The patient sits upright and holds a cassette against the side of the face, but parallel to the midline sagittal plane, and the chin is raised.
- The X-ray tubehead is positioned beneath the lower border of the mandible on the opposite side, aiming slightly upwards directly at the area of interest.

## Children

- The child is usually rotated through 90° in the chair, so the shoulder is supported and the cassette and head can be rested on the chair.
- The X-ray tubehead is positioned along the line of the occlusal plane, aiming through the radiographic keyhole.

## If using a carer or relative to help

- Ensure he or she is wearing a protective lead apron.
- The carer can hold the cassette and reassure the patient.
- Positioning of the X-ray tubehead is identical to other oblique laterals.
