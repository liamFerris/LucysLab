# Module 12: How to take bisected angle technique periapical radiographs

*Practical techniques*

## What you should be able to do

- Define the terms periapical and bisected angle.
- Describe the clinical indications for periapical radiography.
- Describe the theory of the bisected angle technique.
- Describe the practical techniques.
- Explain the technique's advantages and disadvantages.

## What is bisected angle periapical radiography?

<mark>Periapical radiography is designed to show individual teeth and the tissues around the apices.</mark>

<mark>With the bisected angle technique, the X-ray beam is aimed perpendicular to an imaginary line which bisects the angle between the long axis of the image receptor and the long axis of the tooth.</mark>

## Clinical indications for taking a periapical radiograph

- Detection of apical infection/inflammation and other diseases
- Assessment of periodontal status
- After trauma to the teeth and associated alveolar bone
- Assessment of root shape and number before extractions
- Before, during and after endodontics
- Before and after apical surgery
- Post-operative evaluation of implants

## Basic theory

In some cases it is not possible to place the image receptor parallel to the tooth, so the two will be at an angle to each other. To prevent geometrical distortion of the image, you must imagine a line which cuts this angle in half, and aim the X-ray beam so that it is at 90° to this imaginary line.

In other words, you must bisect the angle between the tooth and the receptor and aim the X-ray beam at a right angle to the bisecting line. (This technique is best understood from images: consult the relevant slides.)

## Setting up phosphor plate/film holders and beam aiming devices

- Select the appropriate biteblock or holder.
- For anterior teeth, insert a small or large intra-oral phosphor plate or film packet with its long axis vertical.
- For posterior teeth, insert a large intra-oral phosphor plate or film packet with its long axis horizontal.
- Connect the metal arm (anterior or posterior) and the aiming ring, if one is being used.

## Advantages of the bisected angle technique

- The technique is reasonably comfortable.
- Positioning is relatively simple.
- If all angulations are correctly assessed, the image should be adequate (but NOT ideal) for most diagnostic purposes.

## Disadvantages of the bisected angle technique

- The many variables can frequently result in a distorted image.
- The horizontal and vertical angles have to be assessed.
- Incorrect vertical angulation will result in elongation or foreshortening of the image.
- Periodontal bone levels are poorly shown.
- Crowns are distorted, preventing detection of approximal caries.
- Incorrect horizontal angulation will result in overlapping of crowns and roots.
- Coning off (cone cutting) is common.
- Reproducible images are not possible.
- The shadow of the zygomatic bone is superimposed over the roots of the upper molars, and the buccal roots are foreshortened.
