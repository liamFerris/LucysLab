*Quality assurance*

## What is a Quality Assurance programme?

A <mark>Quality Assurance</mark> programme is <mark>"an organised effort by the staff operating a facility to ensure that diagnostic images produced by the facility are of sufficiently high quality so that they consistently provide adequate diagnostic information at the lowest possible cost and with the least possible exposure of the patient or staff to radiation."</mark>

In other words, <mark>it is a system of procedures and checks to help ensure that the quality of the radiographs you produce is excellent, every single time. It is largely "written down common sense".</mark>

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <mark>Quality control</mark> | The specific measures for ensuring and verifying the quality of radiographs produced. |
| <mark>Quality assurance (QA)</mark> | The arrangements to ensure that the quality control procedures are effective and that they lead to relevant change and improvement. |
| <mark>Quality audit</mark> | The process of external reassurance and assessment that quality control and quality assurance mechanisms are satisfactory and that they work effectively. |

## Aims of a QA programme

- Produce diagnostic radiographs to a consistently high standard.
- Reduce the number of repeat radiographs.
- Determine all sources of error and allow their correction.
- Increase efficiency.
- Decrease costs.
- Ensure radiation doses to patients and staff are kept as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP).

## Main areas to be addressed by a QA programme

- Image quality (digital and film).
- Imaging receptor, processing and display equipment.
- Patient dose and X-ray equipment.
- Working procedures.
- Staff training and updating.
- Audit.
