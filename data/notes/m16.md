## Panoramic tomography: the concept

<mark>A tomograph is an image of a 'slice' of the patient. A Dental Panoramic Tomograph (DPT) is so called because it shows a wide view (panorama) of the slice which contains the patient's jaws.</mark>

## Geometric principles

- The tomographic layer (slice) is created by the **circular movement of the equipment around the patient's head**.
- The film or sensor passes around the **front of the patient's face**, whilst the X-ray tubehead passes around the **back of the patient's head**.
- The X-ray beam is a **vertical slit**, angled **upwards at about 8 degrees**.
- Structures which lie within the 3D **horseshoe-shaped slice (focal trough)** will be in focus and slightly magnified.
- Structures outside the horseshoe will appear blurred and distorted in size.

## Clinical indications for panoramic tomography

- Where a bony lesion or unerupted tooth is of a size or position that precludes its complete demonstration on intra-oral radiographs.
- Grossly neglected mouth.
- As part of an assessment of periodontal bone support.
- As part of an orthodontic assessment using clinical criteria.
- Assessment of third molars prior to planned surgical intervention.
- Mandibular fractures.
- Antral disease.
- Destructive bone disease affecting the TMJ.
- Pre-implant planning.

## Patient preparation

- Confirm the patient's identity.
- Ask the patient to remove earrings, necklaces, dentures, orthodontic appliances, spectacles and hair ornaments which may be in the path of the X-ray beam.
- Explain the procedure and equipment to the patient and, if necessary, use a test exposure to show the machine movement.

## Panoramic technique variations

### Edentulous patients

- <mark>Do not use the bite-peg.</mark>
- <mark>Rest the chin on the chin support.</mark>
- <mark>Centre the canine positioning light beam on the corner of the mouth.</mark>

### Field limitation techniques

- Only pre-selected parts of the patient are exposed and captured on the final image.

### TMJ programmes

- Enable the condyles to be imaged with the mouth closed and with the mouth open.

## Advantages of dental panoramic tomographs

- A large area is imaged and all tissues within the in-focus layer are displayed on one image, even if the patient cannot open their mouth, which is useful following trauma.
- Patient positioning is relatively simple.
- The image is a useful teaching aid for patients.
- Patient movement in the vertical plane only distorts the part of the image being produced at that instant.
- Useful for overall evaluation of periodontal status and as part of an orthodontic assessment.
- Specific TMJ programmes are available to image the condyles.
- Field limitation techniques are available.
- Relatively low-dose examination.
- Compatible with solid-state technology.

## Disadvantages of dental panoramic tomographs

- The tomographic image represents only a section or layer of the patient, so structures or abnormalities not in the focal trough may not be evident.
- Soft tissue shadows, ghost shadows and air shadows can all overlie the required hard tissue structures.
- Equipment movement will result in some loss of image quality.
- Not suitable for children under six years of age.
- The shape of some patients' jaws does not conform to the shape of the in-focus layer (focal trough).

## Ideal image quality criteria for panoramic radiographs

- All the upper and lower teeth, and their supporting alveolar bone, should be clearly demonstrated.
- The whole of the mandible should be included.
- Magnification in the vertical and horizontal planes should be equal.
- The right and left molar teeth should be equal in mesio-distal dimension.
- The density across the radiograph should be uniform, with no air shadow above the tongue creating a radiolucent (black) band over the roots of the upper teeth.
- The image of the hard palate should appear above the apices of the upper teeth.
- Ideally, only the slightest ghost shadows of the contralateral angle of the mandible and the cervical spine should be evident.
- There should be no evidence of artefactual shadows due to dentures, earrings or other jewellery.
- The patient identification label should not obscure any of the clinically relevant features.
- The image should be clearly labelled with the patient's name and date of examination.
- The image should be clearly marked with a Right and/or Left letter for correct orientation.
