# Module 8: What is an X-ray image?

*Understanding the different X-ray images and their limitations*

## What you should be able to do

- Describe the shadows that make up traditional 2D X-ray images (digital or film-captured).
- Explain the various factors that can affect traditional 2D X-ray images (digital and film-captured).
- Describe alternative types of X-ray images, including panoramic tomographs and 3D digital images.
- Explain the limitations of the various different types of X-ray images.

## Shadows

There are three main types of shadow which make up a radiographic image:

| Shadow | Appearance | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiolucent** | <mark>Black areas</mark> | <mark>X-ray photons have passed through the patient (no absorption) and hit the film</mark> |
| **Radiopaque** | <mark>White areas</mark> | <mark>X-ray photons have been totally absorbed by the patient</mark> |
| **Mixture** | <mark>Grey areas</mark> | <mark>There are varying degrees of absorption</mark> |

## Factors that can affect an X-ray image

- Object material
- Object thickness or density
- Object shape
- X-ray beam intensity (which depends on exposure factors)
- Position of the object, film and X-ray beam

## Limitations of the different types of X-ray image

### 2-dimensional digital images

- Two-dimensional representation of a 3D object
- Affected by geometrical distortion
- Affected by software image processing

### Plain films, e.g. conventional periapicals and bitewings

- Two-dimensional representations of 3D objects
- Affected by exposure factors
- Affected by geometrical distortions
- Affected by processing faults

### Tomographs or "sectional radiographs"

<mark>(NB: dental panoramic radiographs are tomographs.)</mark>

- Only represent a slice of the patient, not a general overview
- Blurred, because the X-ray tubehead and film are moving
- Affected by patient shape and positioning
- Complex: the required hard-tissue images are overlaid with soft tissues, equipment ghost shadows and air shadows
- Affected by processing faults
- Affected by processing factors
- Resolution is relatively poor because an indirect-action film-screen image receptor is used

### 3-dimensional digital images

Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) equipment creates images representing a slice of the patient in different planes. It can produce a variety of views, including:

- <mark>sagittal, coronal and axial views</mark>
- <mark>panoramic views</mark>
- <mark>3-dimensional views</mark>

<mark>Multiple complex 3D volumetric and reformatted images can be created, requiring detailed 3D anatomical knowledge.</mark>

**Limitations:**

- Resolution dependent on voxel size
- Affected by patient shape, positioning and movement
- Metallic objects can create star artefacts
- Affected by exposure factors and computer processing
