# Module 18: How do you take occlusal radiographs?

*Practical techniques*

## What you should be able to do

- Define the term occlusal radiography and name the different occlusal projections.
- Describe the clinical indications for the different occlusal radiographs.
- Describe the geometrical requirements and practical techniques for the various occlusal projections.
- Describe the ideal image quality criteria for the different views.

## What is occlusal radiography?

<mark>Occlusal radiography describes those intra-oral techniques where the image receptor (phosphor plate or film packet) is placed in the occlusal plane. It is not possible to take occlusal radiographs using CCD sensors.</mark>

| Maxillary occlusal projections | Mandibular occlusal projections |
|---|---|
| Upper standard occlusal | Lower 90° occlusal (or true occlusal) |
| Upper oblique occlusal | Lower 45° occlusal |
| | Lower oblique occlusal (rarely used and not described in this module) |

## Upper standard occlusal

### Clinical indications

- Evaluation of the size and extent of lesions, such as cysts, in the anterior maxilla.
- Periapical assessment of the upper anterior teeth in patients who cannot tolerate periapical films.
- As part of an orthodontic assessment, to detect the presence of unerupted canines, supernumeraries and odontomes.
- Dento-alveolar trauma to the anterior maxilla.
- As the midline view when locating unerupted canines, supernumerary teeth and odontomes.

### Geometrical requirements and practical technique

- Patient seated with the head supported and the occlusal plane horizontal.
- **Use a protective thyroid shield.**
- Insert the film packet/phosphor plate into the mouth with the long axis crossways in adults, and antero-posteriorly in children.
- Patient bites gently together.
- X-ray tubehead positioned above the patient in the midline, aiming downwards at an angle of 65°–70° to the image receptor.
- For small children, place a periapical film packet/phosphor plate flat in the mouth, in the occlusal plane, with the X-ray beam angled at 50°.

### Quality criteria

- Apices of the upper incisor teeth clearly defined.
- Minimal elongation/foreshortening.
- If present, the crowns and apices of unerupted canines should be included.
- First and second molars should be included.
- No geometrical distortion due to incorrect beam angulation or image receptor bending.
- Right and left should be comparable.
- Contrast and density should be such that enamel, dentine and pulp can be differentiated.

## Upper oblique occlusal

### Clinical indications

- Periapical assessment of the upper posterior teeth, in adults who cannot tolerate periapical films.
- Evaluation of the size and extent of lesions, such as cysts, in the posterior maxilla.
- Assessment of the condition of the antral floor.
- Assessment of the position of roots displaced into the antrum during attempted extraction.
- To assess cleft palate.
- Assessment of fractures of the posterior teeth and/or alveolar bone.

### Geometrical requirements and practical technique

- Patient seated with the head supported and the occlusal plane horizontal.
- **Use a protective thyroid shield.**
- Insert the film packet/phosphor plate into the mouth with the long axis antero-posteriorly, to the side of the mouth under investigation.
- Patient bites gently together.
- X-ray tubehead positioned to the side of the patient's face, aiming downwards through the cheek at an angle of 65°–70° to the image receptor, centred on the area of interest.

### Quality criteria

- Apices of the upper posterior teeth being investigated clearly defined.
- Minimal foreshortening/elongation.
- All posterior teeth on one side should be included.
- The antral floor should be shown.
- No additional geometrical distortion due to incorrect beam angulation or film bending.
- Contrast and density should be such that enamel, dentine and pulp can be differentiated.

## Lower 90° occlusal (true occlusal)

### Clinical indications

- Detection of calculi in the submandibular duct.

> **Lucy's note:** Calculi here means salivary stones.

- Assessment of the bucco-lingual expansion of lesions affecting the mandible.
- Assessment of mandibular tori.
- Assessment of the bucco-lingual position of unerupted teeth, including wisdom teeth.
- Assessment of horizontal displacement of fractures of the mandible.
- As part of implant assessment.

### Geometrical requirements and practical technique

- Insert the film packet/phosphor plate centrally into the mouth with the long axis antero-posteriorly or crossways, depending on the clinical indication.
- Patient bites gently together.
- Patient then tips the head backwards as far as is comfortable.
- X-ray tubehead placed below the patient's chin, in the midline, at an angle of 90° to the image receptor; or
- X-ray tubehead placed below the body of the mandible on the side of interest, at an angle of 90° to the image receptor.

### Quality criteria

- The first molar teeth should be included.
- No geometrical distortion of the image due to incorrect beam angulation or image receptor bending.
- The body of the mandible on each side should be equidistant from the midline and should appear symmetrical.
- The required contrast and density will depend on the clinical indication. To demonstrate salivary calculi, a soft tissue exposure should be used.

## Lower 45° occlusal

### Clinical indications

- Assessment of dento-alveolar trauma, in conjunction with periapicals.
- Evaluation of the size and extent of lesions in the anterior mandible.
- As an alternative to periapicals in patients who cannot tolerate them.
- Assessment of vertical displacement of fractures of the mandible.

### Geometrical requirements and practical technique

- Patient seated with the head supported and the occlusal plane horizontal.
- Insert the film packet/phosphor plate centrally into the mouth with the long axis antero-posteriorly or crossways.
- Patient bites gently together.
- X-ray tubehead is positioned in the midline, centred through the chin point, at an angle of 45° to the image receptor.

### Quality criteria

- Apices of the lower incisor teeth should be clearly defined.
- Minimal foreshortening/elongation of the incisor teeth.
- The lower border of the mandible should be visible.
- No geometrical distortion of the image due to incorrect beam angulation or image receptor bending.
- Contrast and density should be such that enamel, dentine and pulp can be differentiated.
