*Practical techniques*

## What is a bitewing?

<mark>Bitewings take their name from the original technique, which required the patient to bite on a small wing attached to an intra-oral film packet.</mark> <mark>This technique is designed to image the crowns of the premolar and molar teeth on one side of the jaws.</mark> <mark>The tabs are nowadays replaced with digital sensor/film holders incorporating beam aiming devices.</mark>

## Clinical indications for taking a bitewing radiograph

- <mark>Detection of dental caries</mark>
- <mark>Monitoring the progression of dental caries</mark>
- <mark>Assessment of existing restorations</mark>
- <mark>Assessment of periodontal status</mark>

## Geometrical requirements

The film should be placed parallel to the arch, and the tubehead aimed between the contact points of the teeth. The tubehead should also be aimed slightly downwards, at between 5° and 8°.

Check the shape of the patient's arch before placing the image receptor. For patients with a square arch, you may have to use two phosphor plates/films to make sure that the X-ray beam passes through the contact points of the teeth.

## Traditional bitewing technique (bite tabs)

### Advantages

- Simple in both adults and children.
- The tabs (wings) are inexpensive and disposable, so no extra control of infection measures are required.
- Can be performed easily using phosphor plates or film.

### Disadvantages

- Assessment of the vertical and horizontal angulations is arbitrary and operator-dependent, so contact areas are easily overlapped.
- Coning off (cone cutting) of the anterior part of the image receptor is common.
- The tongue can easily displace the phosphor plate or film packet.
- Not suitable for solid-state digital sensors.
- The resulting radiographs are not reproducible.

## Bitewings using an image receptor holder

### Advantages

- Simple.
- All types of digital sensors and film packets can be used.
- The image receptor is held firmly in position and cannot be displaced.
- The horizontal and vertical position of the tubehead is determined by the beam aiming device, so coning off (cone cutting) is avoided.
- Holders are autoclavable or disposable.

### Disadvantages

- The position of the holder in the mouth is still operator-dependent, so images are not 100 per cent reproducible. This makes them not perfect for monitoring the progression of caries.
- Positioning some of the available sensor/film holders can be uncomfortable for the patient.
- Some holders are relatively expensive.
- Holders can be difficult to use on children.

### Ideal bitewing image quality criteria

- **Contact points open:** the contact points between adjacent teeth are open, with no overlapping, so the approximal surfaces can be checked for caries. Overlap is caused by incorrect horizontal angulation.
- **Occlusal plane centred:** the occlusal plane runs roughly horizontally across the centre of the image, so the upper and lower teeth are shown in roughly equal amounts.
- **Crestal bone visible:** the alveolar crestal bone between the teeth is visible, so periodontal bone levels can be assessed.
- **Contrast and density:** enamel, dentine and pulp can be told apart, so caries can be detected.
