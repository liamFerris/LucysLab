# Module 17: What's wrong?

*Panoramic radiographic image faults*

## What you should be able to do

- Explain how panoramic image quality is assessed and rated.
- Recognise and describe the main patient preparation and positioning faults on panoramic images.
- Explain how to correct the main patient preparation and positioning faults.
- Recognise and describe other specific digital and film-based panoramic image faults.

## Panoramic quality criteria

- <mark>All the upper and lower teeth and their supporting alveolar bone should be clearly demonstrated.</mark>
- The whole of the mandible should be included.
- <mark>Magnification in the vertical and horizontal planes should be equal.</mark>
- The right and left molar teeth should be equal in mesio-distal dimension.
- The density across the radiograph should be uniform, <mark>with no air shadow above the tongue creating a radiolucent (black) band over the roots of the upper teeth.</mark>
- The image of the hard palate should appear above the apices of the teeth.
- <mark>Ideally only the slightest ghost shadows of the contralateral angle of the mandible and the cervical spine should be evident.</mark>
- There should be no evidence of artefactual shadows due to dentures, earrings and other jewellery.
- The patient identification label should not obscure any of the above features.
- The image should be clearly labelled with the patient's name and the date of examination.
- The image should be clearly marked with a right and/or left letter to ensure correct orientation.

## Subjective quality rating of radiographs

| Quality rating | Basis of decision |
|---|---|
| <mark>A – Diagnostically acceptable</mark> | No errors, or minimal errors, in patient preparation, exposure, positioning, image processing or image reconstruction, and of sufficient image quality to answer the clinical question. |
| <mark>N – Diagnostically not acceptable</mark> | Errors in patient preparation, exposure, positioning, image processing or image reconstruction which render the image diagnostically unacceptable. |

## Errors of patient preparation (and corrective action)

- Failure to remove jewellery will result in ghost shadows obscuring the image. Pay close attention to earrings, necklaces, spectacles, hair ornaments and piercings.
- Failure to remove dentures will also cause ghost shadows.
- Using a lead apron during panoramic radiography will result in a very large ghost shadow.

### The three golden rules of patient preparation

1. Ask the patient to remove earrings, necklaces, dentures, orthodontic appliances, spectacles and hair ornaments.
2. Explain the procedure and equipment to the patient.
3. Do not use a protective lead apron.

## Errors of patient positioning (and corrective action)

| Fault | What causes it and how it looks | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Cervical spine ghosting | Spinal shadows will be evident if the patient's neck is extended, or if they are placed too far forwards in the machine. | Ensure the patient is positioned so that their spine is straight. |
| Antero-posterior errors | If the patient is positioned too far forward or back, the anterior teeth will be blurred and distorted. | Ask the patient to bite their incisors edge-to-edge on the bite peg. |
| Horizontal plane errors | If the patient's head is rotated to one side, the image will be blurred and distorted in size. | Use the vertical light beam marker as a guide to adjust the head position, so that the midline sagittal plane is vertical and the head is not rotated. |
| Vertical plane errors | If the head is tipped up or down, the occlusal plane will be distorted (a "smiley" or "grumpy" face). | Use the horizontal light beam marker to ensure the Frankfort plane is horizontal. |
| Unwanted air shadow | If the patient does not keep their tongue pressed to the roof of their mouth, a dark air shadow will overlie the image. | Ask the patient to press their tongue against the roof of the mouth throughout the exposure. |
| Movement artefacts | If the patient moves, sharp jumps and blurring can appear on the image. | Ask the patient to remain still throughout the exposure. |

### The six golden rules of patient positioning

1. <mark>Ensure the patient is positioned so that their spine is straight.</mark>
2. <mark>Ensure the patient bites edge-to-edge on the bite peg.</mark>
3. <mark>Ensure the mid-sagittal plane is vertical and the head is not rotated.</mark>
4. <mark>Ensure the Frankfort plane is horizontal and the head is not tipped up or down.</mark>
5. <mark>Ensure the tongue is pressed against the roof of the mouth.</mark>
6. <mark>Ensure the patient remains still throughout the exposure.</mark>

## Other panoramic image faults

### Digital images

| Appearance | Cause |
|---|---|
| Image too dark | Overexposed |
| Image too pale | Underexposed |
| Image badly distorted | Software malfunction |

### Film images

| Appearance | Cause |
|---|---|
| Image too dark | Overexposed, overdeveloped or fogged |
| Image too pale | Underexposed or underdeveloped |
| Two images on one film | Film exposed twice (double exposure) |
| Image marked | Processing error or dirty intensifying screen |
| Image with "lightning marks" | Static |
